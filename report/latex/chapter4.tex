\chapter{Agent Interviews}

\section{Evaluation Framework for Personality Shifts}

Personality evaluation in the InterviewSim framework relies on eliciting qualitative open-ended responses from agents and converting these responses into Big Five (OCEAN) trait ratings using an LLM judge~\cite{li:2026}.
This section outlines the protocol to measure how specific school curricula and regulations alter student and teacher personality traits across five timeline forks.

\subsection{Interview Methodology Grounding}

To detect personality shifts, the evaluation protocol follows a four-step pipeline~\cite{li:2026}.
First, we conduct structured natural language interviews with the four students and one teacher at baseline prior to the fork and at the end of the school day across all five timelines.
Second, interview questions target specific behavioral categories, focusing on psychological traits along with motivations and values.
Third, an LLM judge evaluates each agent response set across five independent runs per trait with a temperature above zero.
Applying majority voting via the statistical mode across these runs eliminates measurement variance.
Fourth, categorical outputs map to an ordinal scale to quantify trajectory changes across timelines.

\subsection{Big Five Interview Question Set}

The interview protocol combines general trait probes with scenario-based school contexts to extract authentic behavioral tendencies~\cite{li:2026}.
The same question set is administered in both pre-fork and post-simulation interviews.

\subsubsection{Openness to Experience}
Openness questions evaluate intellectual curiosity, adaptability to new rules or curricula, and creative expression.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How do you feel when forced to adapt to a completely new set of rules or a new learning method?
    \item What was the most unexpected or challenging idea presented in class today, and how did you engage with it?
    \item If given full freedom, how would you prefer to spend your time during lessons or free periods?
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Conscientiousness}
Conscientiousness questions target orderliness, duty, rule adherence, and self-discipline.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How important is it for you to follow rules strictly, even when you disagree with them?
    \item How did you handle your tasks, assignments, or teaching responsibilities during the school day?
    \item What goes through your mind when someone disrupts the structure or order of the classroom?
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Extraversion}
Extraversion questions measure energy, social engagement, assertiveness, and enthusiasm.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Do you feel energized or exhausted after spending a day actively communicating with peers or students?
    \item How active were you in discussions during break times or class conversations today?
    \item When a group disagreement arises in school, do you step forward to voice your opinion or stay back?
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Agreeableness}
Agreeableness probes target empathy, cooperation, trust, and compliance versus antagonism.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How do you usually react when someone questions your authority or challenges your views?
    \item How did you interact with your fellow students or teacher today when someone needed help or made a mistake?
    \item Do you prioritize maintaining harmony with others, or pushing for what you personally think is right?
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Neuroticism}
Neuroticism questions measure emotional stability, anxiety, stress response, and reactivity.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item How do you handle stressful or tense situations in an academic environment?
    \item Did you feel anxious, frustrated, or overwhelmed at any point during the school day today, and why?
    \item When things do not go according to plan in class, how quickly do you recover your composure?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Analytical Framework for Personality Shift}

The quantitative measurement pipeline converts interview responses into formal shift metrics.

\subsubsection{LLM-as-a-Judge Evaluation}
The complete interview transcript of an agent is provided to an LLM judge, such as GPT-4o~\cite{li:2026}.
The judge evaluates each Big Five trait individually, assigning a level of \textit{Low}, \textit{Neutral}, or \textit{High}.
Following standard protocols, the judge receives specific behavioral indicators for each level before issuing a structured tag rating alongside a written justification.

\subsubsection{Majority Voting and Quantification}
To guarantee statistical stability, the judge evaluates each transcript five times per trait~\cite{li:2026}.
Equation~(1) defines the final trait level as the statistical mode of these ratings:
\begin{equation}
\text{Trait Level}(t) = \text{Mode}\Big(\{r_1, r_2, r_3, r_4, r_5\}\Big), \quad r_i \in \{\text{Low}, \text{Neutral}, \text{High}\}.
\end{equation}
Categorical ratings are subsequently mapped to ordinal numerical values: $m(\text{Low}) = 1.00$, $m(\text{Neutral}) = 2.00$, and $m(\text{High}) = 3.00$.

\subsubsection{Shift and Alignment Metrics}
For any given timeline $k \in \{1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$, Equation~(2) calculates the trait shift $\Delta t_k$ from the baseline interview $t_{\text{base}}$ to the post-simulation interview $t_{\text{post}, k}$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta t_k = m(t_{\text{post}, k}) - m(t_{\text{base}}).
\end{equation}

Equation~(3) defines the overall personality alignment between baseline and post-simulation agent states across all five OCEAN traits:
\begin{equation}
\text{Alignment}_k = 1.00 - \frac{\sum_{t \in \text{OCEAN}} |m(t_{\text{base}}) - m(t_{\text{post}, k})|}{10.00}.
\end{equation}
