\chapter{Summary and Limitations}
\label{limitations}

Across all five simulated party environments, student agents' expressed Big
Five profiles shifted in the same direction — higher Extraversion,
Agreeableness, Conscientiousness, and Open-Mindedness, and lower Negative
Emotionality — relative to the pre-simulation baseline, with reliable
statistical significance concentrated in Open-Mindedness (all five parties),
Agreeableness (AfD, Gr\"une, Linke), Extraversion (AfD, SPD), and Neg.\
Emotionality (Gr\"une, Linke). Linke produced the strongest pro-social
effect (largest Agreeableness gain, largest Neg.\ Emotionality drop),
consistent with its low-pressure, non-selective programme, while CDU
produced the fewest significant shifts overall, consistent with its
stability- and discipline-oriented programme. SPD's environment stood out
for the largest Open-Mindedness gain, and AfD unexpectedly combined the
largest Extraversion gain with significant Agreeableness and Open-Mindedness
gains despite its traditional, discipline-focused platform.

This last result, together with the fact that no domain reverses sign in any
environment, is a reason for caution rather than a straightforward finding
about AfD's programme specifically. Three limitations bear on interpretation:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Sample size.} The paired analysis rests on $n=4$ students per
environment. This is enough to detect very large effects (Open-Mindedness,
$d>1.6$ throughout) but underpowered for the medium effects seen in
Conscientiousness, and it limits the Wilcoxon test to a small set of
possible $p$-values, several of which never reach $p<0.05$ regardless of
effect size.
\item \textbf{Uniform direction across parties.} Every environment shifted
every domain the same way, which is consistent with a general "day in a
structured simulated school with peers" effect that is at least partly
independent of the specific party programme. The between-party differences
we report are therefore best read as differences in \emph{magnitude} on top
of a shared baseline exposure effect, not as fully independent, party-caused
effects.
\item \textbf{Single simulated day and LLM-judge measurement.} Each
timeline covers one school day, and personality levels are inferred by an
LLM judge from interview transcripts rather than measured with a validated
self-report instrument administered to the agents directly (Section~3.4.1).
Majority voting over five judge runs stabilises the categorical rating, but
the underlying scale is coarse (Low/Neutral/High mapped to 1/2/3) and may not
capture smaller or slower-forming personality changes that a longer
simulation or a directly administered BFI-2 might reveal.
\end{enumerate}

Future work could extend the simulation to multiple school days per
environment to test whether party-specific differences grow relative to the
shared baseline effect, increase the number of student agents per timeline
to improve statistical power (particularly for Conscientiousness), and
compare the LLM-judge ratings against a directly administered BFI-2
questionnaire on the agents as a validity check.
